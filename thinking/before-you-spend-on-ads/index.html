Campaign planning / 3 minute read

What to clarify before spending on ads.

Six questions that turn “we should run ads” into a usable campaign brief.

In this guide

Demand Campaigns

An advertising budget is easier to use well when the business decisions around it are made first. Before choosing an audience or writing an ad, work through the offer, destination, and follow-up together. The answers do not need to fill a long document. They need to be specific enough to guide the campaign.

Start with one offer and one action.

“Promote the business” leaves too many decisions open. Choose the service, product, or introductory offer the campaign will focus on, then name the action you want someone to take. A quote request and a purchase need different messages, destinations, and follow-up.

Write a sentence that names the offer, intended audience, and next step. If several audiences or offers compete for the same sentence, choose the first priority before expanding the brief.

Define who can actually use the offer.

Describe the customer’s situation, need, and ability to take the next step. Include the market you can serve and any practical limits. For a local service, location matters. For a B2B offer, the buyer’s business problem and role may matter more than a broad industry label.

Treat this as a starting hypothesis. The campaign should help you learn whether the offer and message reach suitable people.

Check the destination before buying the visit.

Read the destination as someone seeing the offer for the first time. Can they understand what is included, whether it fits them, and what happens next? Test the form, checkout, or booking route on a phone as well as a larger screen.

If the destination needs development, creative production, or substantial tracking work, identify that scope before launch. An ad management fee does not automatically cover those tasks.

Separate the budgets and define the evidence.

List the agency fee, launch or onboarding cost, media budget, and any production or technical costs separately. Then agree what you can measure reliably. A calendar click is different from a completed booking, and a submitted inquiry still needs to be assessed for fit.

Establish the starting position and a review window before making changes. Evaluate results in the context of spend, the buying cycle, and the volume of useful actions.

Give the next step an owner.

Decide who receives an inquiry, who follows up, and who approves campaign changes. A campaign brief should describe what the business will do after the click as clearly as what the ad will say.

  • Offer: what exactly are we promoting?
  • Audience: who needs it, and where can we serve them?
  • Action: what should they do next?
  • Destination: is that action working?
  • Budget and measurement: what is separate, and what counts?
  • Ownership: who approves, responds, and reviews?
