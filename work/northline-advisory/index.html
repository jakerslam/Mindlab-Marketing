The brief

Help business owners understand the advisory offer before asking them to book a conversation.

Northline Advisory is a fictional business used to demonstrate our thinking. The brief: help an advisory firm explain its work to owners preparing for their next stage of growth. This is an original Mindlab concept, not a client engagement.

Sample concept. No campaign was run and no client results are claimed.

Northline AdvisorySample concept

Three questions worth publishing.

Each content theme gives the owner something useful to consider before a sales conversation.

Theme 01 / Readiness

Are you ready to grow, or already stretched?

Article outline: name the opportunity; review capacity, ownership, and cash requirements; identify what must be in place; end with one readiness question for the owner.

Theme 02 / Tradeoffs

What will you stop doing to make room?

Social series: one post on competing priorities, one on the cost of maintaining them all, and one on choosing a decision criterion before evaluating options.

Theme 03 / Next steps

What can you learn before committing?

A short checklist on the assumptions to test, the information to gather, and the person responsible for the next decision.

Northline AdvisorySample concept

A sample post, ready to read.

Writing example for a professional-services company page.

Social post

Before adding a new growth priority, name the tradeoff.

A new market, a new service, and a new hire can all sound like the next right move. The question is whether the business can support them at the same time. Before committing, write down three things: what this move is meant to change, what it will require from the business, and what you will pause to make room. If those answers are still vague, the next step may be a better brief rather than a bigger plan. What decision would become easier if the tradeoff were explicit?

The reasoning

Why these choices?

  1. 01 /

    Start with the buyer's decision.

    Focus the message on owners weighing their next business move. Describe the decision they face before introducing the advisory process.

  2. 02 /

    Build one consistent message.

    Use a positioning idea—clarity for your next chapter—to connect the website introduction, service explanation, and content themes.

  3. 03 /

    Turn expertise into useful questions.

    Organize content around readiness, tradeoffs, and next steps. Each theme gives the business a practical way to share its thinking.

Proposed measurement

What we would
look for next.

This is an unlaunched demonstration. A real engagement would first establish the baseline and agree what a useful inquiry looks like.

  • Whether prospective buyers can explain the offer in their own words
  • Relevant visits and responses to the published content
  • Suitable inquiries and progression to a business conversation

The concept shows how positioning can guide content. It has not run as a campaign and has no client results or performance claims.
