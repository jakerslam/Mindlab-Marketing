# Mindlab Solutions: B2B and B2C launch strategy, version 0.3

Updated November 5, 2026. Proposed USD prices and delivery assumptions require validation through buyer conversations and actual delivery records.

## Confirmed direction

- Audience: both B2B and B2C businesses, including professional services.
- Current delivery strengths: ads, content, and branding plans.
- Still open: preferred niche, geography, team capacity, advertising platforms, content formats, and existing client work/results available to show with permission.

Position Mindlab around brand strategy, content, and focused paid campaigns. A useful customer journey is: clarify the offer → build trust → promote the right offer → assess commercial results. Keep one agency and one core service menu, with delivery adapted to each client's buyers and business model.

Recommended positioning draft:

> Mindlab helps businesses clarify their brand, create content that connects, and run focused campaigns that bring the right customers closer.

Keep both audiences eligible. For initial outreach, test one specific B2B segment and one specific B2C segment where the founders have access or relevant work. Possible interview groups include consulting/advisory firms on the B2B side and established consumer service businesses or independent consumer brands on the B2C side. These are hypotheses to validate.

| Application | B2B | B2C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand strategy | Explain expertise, differentiation, buyer fit, and reasons to trust the firm | Explain customer benefit, identity, experience, and reasons to choose the offer |
| Content | Expert insights, case studies, buyer education, and sales support | Product/service education, demonstrations, customer stories, and useful promotional content |
| Campaign goal | Suitable inquiries, held sales conversations, and opportunities | Qualified bookings, purchases, repeat customers, or suitable inquiries, depending on the business |

Agree the actual content format and campaign objective during scoping. B2C product sales and consumer service bookings need different conversion paths and economics.

## Initial customer profile

Use these proposed filters to qualify an initial prospect list:

- An established business with budget for the selected engagement and any media spend; use company size as a secondary filter.
- A founder, owner, partner, or small marketing team who can approve work and provide useful source material.
- A proven product or service and existing customers.
- A specific commercial problem: unclear differentiation, inconsistent content, or campaigns failing to attract suitable customers.
- Customer contribution and acquisition economics that can support the agency fee and any media spend.
- Capacity to serve new customers and a person responsible for sales follow-up or the purchase/booking experience.

Interview 8–10 businesses across the two initial segments. Ask what the business sells, who buys, why it wins, typical buying time, what channels contribute, what has been tried, and who would own the engagement. Compare response, willingness to pay, access to source material, and delivery effort. Use that evidence to prioritize sales effort while keeping both B2B and B2C within the agency's remit.

## Services and proposed packages

Present three separate buying options. Recommend the one that addresses the buyer's immediate problem. A business with clear positioning may start directly with content or campaigns. Prices remain provisional; new formats and production requirements must be costed before being included.

| Offer | Proposed price | Defined scope | Buyer trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Brand Blueprint** | **$1,250 once** | Up to two stakeholder interviews; review of three competitors; one priority buyer profile; positioning and value proposition; message hierarchy; brand voice guidance; three content themes; a 90-day marketing plan; one presentation and two consolidated revision rounds | The business struggles to explain its difference or market a priority offer |
| **Content Studio** | **$1,000/month + $250 onboarding** | One 45-minute source interview monthly; eight written social posts for one agreed profile or page; one article of up to 1,200 words or one email of up to 800 words; monthly calendar; two consolidated revision rounds; performance review and one monthly call | The business needs consistent, useful content to support its offer |
| **Demand Campaigns** | **$1,250/month + $500 launch** | One ad platform, one product/service offer, one agreed market, up to three active campaigns and $10,000 monthly media spend under management; campaign setup; up to four new text/copy variants monthly; landing-page message brief; weekly optimization; reporting against the agreed conversion goal and one monthly call | The business has a validated offer and wants to test paid acquisition |

Brand Blueprint is a strategy and messaging engagement. Logo creation, visual identity design, original photography, and website implementation require separate scopes. Proposed delivery time: two to three weeks after interviews and required inputs, subject to actual capacity.

Content Studio uses client expertise, customer insight, and approved source material. For B2B, develop expert insights and buyer education; for B2C, develop customer-facing education, stories, and offer content. Agree whether Mindlab provides drafts for approval or publishes through authorized access. This initial price covers writing. Graphic design, video production, photography, daily engagement, direct-message management, additional profiles, email sending/automation, and additional long-form assets require separate scopes. Approvals, factual review, usable visual assets, and available interview time are client responsibilities. Reprice production packages after confirming the team's actual capabilities and costs.

Demand Campaigns includes ad concepts and copy using approved client assets. Custom design, video, landing-page development, ecommerce catalog/feed work, extensive tracking/CRM integration, additional platforms, and media above the management cap require separate scopes. Confirm which platforms and conversion flows the team can confidently deliver before publishing the options.

Ad spend is paid separately by the client. Software and specialist implementation costs are separately itemized. Clients retain ownership of advertising accounts, content, and collected data, subject to agreed licenses and contract terms.

Suggested starting terms to develop into the service agreement: project work at 50% to start and 50% at delivery; recurring work billed monthly in advance; a 90-day initial delivery and learning period with monthly reviews and documented cancellation/renewal terms. Agree approval timelines and change requests before kickoff. Promise defined deliverables and communication standards; establish performance targets from the client's baseline and economics.

After the separate offers are validated, content and campaign management could be purchased together at the sum of their published fees, with one coordinated plan. Track the coordination cost before introducing a bundled price.

## Advertising channel and measurement plan

Select a channel according to the offer, available demand, targeting requirements, creative assets, budget, and the team's expertise.

- **Google Search:** investigate first where buyers already search for the specific service and there is enough relevant demand. Google describes Search campaigns as reaching people actively searching for products and services. [Google campaign guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2567043?hl=en)
- **LinkedIn:** investigate where professional role and company characteristics are important to reaching the buyer. Its targeting options include professional and company attributes. Assess audience size and budget using the actual campaign forecast. [LinkedIn targeting options](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a424655/)

These are examples of channel-selection hypotheses. Validate the appropriate platform for each B2B or B2C client against its buyers, offer, budget, creative requirements, and the team's capabilities. Scope other platforms when that assessment supports them.

Before launch, agree the product/service offer, target buyer, conversion goal, media budget, conversion path, responsible client owner, and feedback process. Establish working measurement with the client's technical owner or a separately scoped implementation partner.

For a lead-based engagement, review inquiry quality, held sales conversations, opportunities, and eventual wins. For a booking- or purchase-based engagement, review completed bookings or purchases, acquisition cost, cancellations/refunds where relevant, and contribution after marketing. Track content engagement and distribution as supporting indicators. Keep conversion definitions consistent with the client's actual buying process.

Google supports importing qualified or converted lead outcomes through its conversion tools. Where appropriate and properly implemented, this can connect campaign reporting with later sales qualification. [Google lead-conversion guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10029210/offline-conversion-imports-faqs)

A 90-day pilot should assess delivery, message response, the agreed conversion goal, and campaign learning. For businesses with a longer sales cycle, continue tracking later opportunities and sales beyond the pilot. Report attribution assumptions and distinguish attributed outcomes from evidence of incremental outcomes.

## Pricing and capacity

Use an internal cost for founder time as well as contractor and client-specific tool costs. Proposed target: approximately 50–60% gross margin after direct delivery costs, before general overhead, sales, administration, and taxes.

Illustrative Content Studio writing-only model:

- Monthly revenue: $1,000.
- Delivery assumption: 16 hours at an internal cost of $50/hour = $800.
- Absorbed client-specific tools/other direct costs: $100.
- Gross profit: $100, or 10%.

Those hours and costs are assumptions. Measure interviews, research, writing, revisions, coordination, and reporting. Reduce scope or adjust the price if actual costs exceed the model.

Two content clients and two campaign clients would produce $4,500 monthly recurring revenue. Assuming 16 delivery hours per content client and 18 per campaign client, that requires 68 monthly delivery hours, plus onboarding, coordination between services, sales, and administration. Confirm available capacity before committing.

For a client's paid acquisition economics, include agency fees, media, setup, and other marketing costs over the relevant buying period. Compare them with contribution profit from incremental customers. For lead-based businesses, use actual close rates and buying cycles to estimate required opportunity volume. For purchase-based businesses, account for fulfillment costs, discounts, refunds, and any repeat-purchase assumptions. State assumptions explicitly.

Homepage flow: shared promise → recognizable problems → relevant work → Brand Blueprint / Content Studio / Demand Campaigns → B2B and B2C application examples → process → team → FAQs → call booking.

Make the problems concrete: an offer that sounds interchangeable, inconsistent content, or campaigns attracting the wrong audience. Show how each package addresses its corresponding problem. Use two short sections to illustrate work for B2B and B2C clients. Add dedicated audience pages later when there is enough relevant work and buyer demand to support them.

Use real previous work with permission and accurate role attribution. Existing proof remains unconfirmed. Where needed, prepare clearly labeled examples: a positioning teardown, an interview-to-content example, and a campaign concept. Include verified results only with supporting context, dates, spend where relevant, and the team's actual role.

Keep the inquiry form short: name, work email, company/website, priority problem, and approximate budget range. Explain the call's purpose and next step. Provide appropriate privacy information for the actual tools and markets used.

## Outreach for the first clients

### Warm introductions and partners

Start with about 15 existing contacts who know founders, business owners, managing partners, or marketing leads in the two selected segments. Explain one buyer problem and the package that solves it.

Develop relationships with about 10 complementary providers, particularly website developers, visual identity designers, and sales consultants. Agree how discovery, scoping, delivery, and handoff work for an actual referral.

### Founder-led publishing

Apply the Content Studio method to Mindlab. Choose one consistent publishing voice and share roughly two useful posts each week: a positioning example, a campaign lesson with context, a buyer objection, or a practical content example. Label the intended client audience clearly when an example applies specifically to B2B or B2C.

Support claims with relevant work and evidence. Contribute thoughtfully to conversations in the selected customer groups. Link suitable posts to an example or offer on the website when useful.

### Targeted prospecting

Research an initial list of 50 businesses: 25 in one B2B segment and 25 in one B2C segment. Capture the decision-maker, offer, likely business issue, a verified observation, and the next action. Use distinct messages and relevant examples for each group. Compare qualified replies, held calls, willingness to pay, and expected delivery effort. Expand the more promising experiments while retaining both audiences within the agency's scope.

For a messaging problem, offer a short positioning review. For inconsistent publishing, offer three content angles based on the business's expertise or customer needs. Discuss campaign management when there is a credible offer, budget, working conversion path, and client capacity to handle the resulting demand.

Sample opening, based on a real observation:

> Hi [Name], I noticed [specific issue] in how [Business] presents [offer] to [customer]. [Verified strength] could support a clearer reason to choose you.
>
> I run Mindlab Solutions. We help businesses sharpen their positioning, create useful content, and run focused campaigns. Would a short outline of three messaging improvements be useful?
>
> [Name] | Mindlab Solutions

Adapt the message to the observed problem. Ask for a small next step. Use no more than two relevant follow-ups and stop on a decline or opt-out.

For US commercial email, include truthful sender information and subject lines, clear identification of the commercial nature, a valid postal address, and an easy opt-out. Honor opt-outs within 10 business days; B2B messages are covered. [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)

Before using the domain for email, configure SPF, DKIM, and DMARC and follow the sending provider's policies. Google recommends all three authentication methods. [Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en)

### Discovery and sales

Track: target account → contacted → positive reply → discovery held → scoped proposal → won/lost → onboarding. Record source, objection, next action, fee, and delivery fit.

In discovery, establish the target customer, current offer, existing proof, buying cycle, economics, decision-maker, available input, and primary marketing obstacle. Recommend one scoped engagement. Content or campaign work can follow a Blueprint when the strategy needs clarification.

For Mindlab's own acquisition, prioritize conversations and message validation initially. Introduce paid promotion when an offer has attracted suitable buyers and there is a clear way to assess the spend.
